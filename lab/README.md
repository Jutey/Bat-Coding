# The Lab
## Free Build Time — No Rules, No Objectives, No Grades

---

## What The Lab Is

After every lesson, the Lab opens.

There's no assignment. No rubric. No right answer.

You have the commands from the lesson. A blank file. And time.

That's it.

---

## Why It Exists

The structured lesson teaches the command.  
The Lab is where you actually learn it.

The moment a kid types something nobody told them to type — makes a joke, tries a weird idea, breaks something on purpose to see what happens — that's the moment real learning starts.

Every "Mad Scientist" achievement, every secret lesson, every moment of genuine curiosity... it comes from Lab time.

---

## Lab Prompts (Optional — Not Required)

After each lesson, there are a few Lab prompts. These are ideas, not requirements.  
The best Lab sessions ignore all of them.

### World 1 Lab Prompts

**After Lesson 1 (echo):**
- Make the computer say something embarrassing
- Make it say something that would confuse a stranger
- Make it tell a joke, but put the punchline on the wrong line

**After Lesson 2 (pause):**
- Build the most dramatic "reveal" you can using pauses
- Try: what if you put 5 pauses in a row? What happens?

**After Lesson 3 (cls):**
- Build a "magic trick" — something appears, then vanishes
- Try putting `cls` at the very beginning. What happens?

**After Lesson 4 (title):**
- Name your terminal something that would make someone nervous
- Change the title 10 times throughout a program. Can you tell a story with just the title bar?

**After Lesson 5 (color):**
- Build a "terminal mood ring" — it changes color based on what's happening
- What's the worst possible color combination? Build it.

**After Lesson 6 (ASCII art):**
- Build your name in ASCII
- Build something that took you more than 20 minutes

**After Lesson 7 (multi-screen output):**
- Write a 5-screen story about something that actually happened to you

**After Lesson 8 (menus):**
- Build a menu for a restaurant, a spaceship, or your bedroom
- Make it look more professional than any menu you've seen in this course

**After Lesson 9 (terminal adventure):**
- Write a story that genuinely surprises you at the end
- Try building a second ending (even though you haven't learned how to branch yet)

---

## The Lab Rule

The only rule: **run it before you stop.**

Whatever you make — run it. See what it does. If it breaks, that counts too.  
Broken programs are museum entries. Broken programs teach debugging.  
Broken programs are progress.

---

## Lab Saves

Everything built in the Lab goes in `/lab/your_work/`.  
No file gets deleted. The museum sees all of it.

Name your files something you'll remember. Filenames are part of the museum.

---

## What The Best Lab Sessions Look Like

A kid who spends 2 minutes in the Lab: runs one of the prompts, moves on.

A kid who spends 30 minutes in the Lab: ignored all the prompts, built something weird, broke it, fixed part of it, left a comment saying "I don't know why this works but it does."

That second kid is going to be a programmer.
